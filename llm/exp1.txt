## 1. Stage: **River**

## 2. Known info

* **Board:** T♠ 9♦ 8♠ 5♦ 2♦
* **Hero (BTN):** 9♣ 8♣ → **two pair (Nines & Eights)**
* **Effective stacks:** Started 100, pot is **39** now; both players have plenty behind.

## 3. Opponent range estimate

**Preflop:** BB flats wide: suited broadways, suited connectors, suited gappers, offsuit broadways, many suited Ax, most pairs.

**Flop (T♠9♦8♠) – BB checks, we check:**
When we check back, BB keeps **all draws**, **all Tx**, **all 9x**, **all 8x**, **pairs**, and **two-pairs/straights** (though J7 / QJ are possible but less likely because they usually bet flop often, but not always).

**Turn (5♦) – BB bets 6 into ~10.5, Hero raises to 17, BB calls:**
BB calling our raise removes a lot of weak air.
Villain’s continuing range includes:

* **Strong made hands:**

  * Tx, 9x, 8x
  * Two-pair: T9, T8, 98
  * Sets: TT/99/88 (but many 99/88 partially blocked by our hand)
* **Straights:** QJ, J7s
* **Strong draws:**

  * **Flush draws:** Ax♦, Kx♦, Qx♦, J♦ combos
  * Combo draws: e.g., Q♦J♠, J♦7♠
  * Pair+FDs (T♦x, 9♦x, 8♦x)
* **Weaker draws that float turn raise:** A♠X♠ sometimes, but less likely because spade draw bricked and BB may fold some.

**River (2♦)** completes **the diamond flush**.
BB checks → generally **denies the strongest flushes**, which often lead river.
Likely hands when checking river:

* **One-pair:** T9? Actually two-pair. But Tx, 9x, 8x.
* **Two-pair:** 98 (same as us), T9, T8
* **Straights (QJ/J7)** sometimes check, fearing flush.
* **Flushes:** Some low diamond flushes may check, fearing a river diamond raise.
* **Bluffs:** Missed spade draws.

---

## 4. Pot odds and equity estimate

We have **two pair** on a board that now has:

* 4-card straight possibilities (QJ, J7)
* Completed backdoor flush (♦♦♦)
* Better two-pair (T9/T8)
* Sets

Our hand is **good but rarely strong enough to value-bet big**, because:

* Worse hands that call: **Tx, 9x, maybe 8x**, occasionally worse two-pair
* Better hands that call/raise: **flushes**, **straights**, **sets**, **better two-pair**

We are almost always **ahead of his bluffing range**.
We almost always get **called by better** if we bet big.
We get some thin value from Tx/9x/8x if we bet small.

But after turn raise + river diamond, most opponents become **much tighter** calling river bets.

So mathematically:
A value bet must be **thin**, like 8–12 chips.
But checking behind guarantees our equity and avoids running into the flushes/straights.

---

## 5. Action reasoning

**Arguments for betting:**

* Villain has many one-pair hands that might call a small bet.
* Our hand is too strong to check-fold, so we never fold.
* A small bet gets value from Tx and 9x.

**Arguments for checking:**

* River completed the flush → many villain continue-range hands improved.
* Worse two-pair is rare; better two-pair and straights exist.
* Turn raise from us polarizes our range, so villain is more likely to call river with strong made hands → we risk value-owning ourselves.
* Our hand has strong showdown value.

**Optimal GTO-style choice:**
**Check back**.
Two pair on a 4-flush/4-straight board is a strong bluff-catcher, not a value hand.

---

## 6. Final decision: **CHECK**

We take our showdown value; betting is thin and often value-cuts against flushes and straights.

---

## 1. Stage: **River**

## 2. Known info

* **Board:** T♥ 4♣ 5♦ K♦ Q♥
* **Hero (BTN):** Q♦ T♠ → **two pair (Queens & Tens)**
* **Pot:** 26 chips
* **River action:** BB leads 9 chips
* **Effective stacks:** Deep (started 100)

## 3. Opponent range estimate

### **Preflop (BB calls BTN 2.5x)**

BB calls wide: all suited broadways, suited connectors, most Tx, mid–low pairs, Ax suited, offsuit broadways, etc.

### **Flop (T 4 5 rainbow) – BB checks, Hero checks**

BB keeps:

* Tx (T9–T2 suited, TJo/TQo sometimes)
* 5x, 4x
* Pocket pairs 22–99
* Straight draws: 76, 63, A3
* Backdoor diamonds

Hero checking back keeps BB’s range wide and disguises our top pair.

### **Turn (K♦) – BB checks, Hero bets 6, BB calls**

BB calling range now includes:

* **Kx** (K9–K2 suited, KJo, KQo sometimes)
* **Tx**, **5x**, **4x**
* **Diamond draws**, especially A♦x, Q♦x, J♦x, 9♦x
* **Straight draws** (A3, 63, 76)
* Some slow-played sets (55/44) but much less likely

### **River (Q♥) – BB leads 9**

BB’s donk lead is usually **thin value or block-bet**, not nutted:

* **Hands that improved to 2-pair or worse pair:**

  * Qx (Q9–Q2 suited, QJo)
  * Kx
  * Tx
* **Missed draws trying to buy a cheap showdown:**

  * 76, 63, A3
  * Some busted diamonds (non-nut)

Hands beating us:

* KQ
* QT (we block)
* Sets (55, 44, TT) — but these almost always raise earlier
* Straights: 76 gets there? **Board is T-4-5-K-Q → 76 does NOT make a straight**
  So straights **miss**.

River Q♥ dramatically reduces villain having strong KQ combos (blocker), improves us to **top two pair**, and kills most draws.

This is almost always a **weak value bet or a bluff**.

---

## 4. Pot odds and equity estimate

BB bets **9 into 26**, giving us:

[
\text{Pot odds} = \frac{9}{26 + 9} = \frac{9}{35} \approx 25.7%
]

We need **~26% equity** to call.

With **top two pair**, our equity vs his leading range is **far higher than 50%**.
We are almost never folding.

The question becomes: **call or raise?**

---

## 5. Action reasoning

### **Why raising is bad**

* Worse hands that bet (Kx, QJ/Q9, Tx) often **fold** to a raise.
* Better hands (KQ, sets) **never fold**.
* Missed draws fold.
  → A raise isolates us vs better hands = **value-owned**.

### **Why calling is optimal**

* BB has tons of weaker value hands: Kx/Qx/T pairs
* And many bluffs: missed diamonds, 76/63/A3
* Our hand is very strong but not strong enough to three-bet river for value
* River donk bet is a classic **block-bet**, a size used to prevent us from betting bigger

→ With top two pair and a polarized villain range, **call maximizes EV**.

---

## 6. Final decision: **CALL**

We beat almost all of villain’s value range, lose only to a few combos, and raising would only get action from hands that crush us.

**Final: CALL**

---

## 1. Stage: **River**

## 2. Known info

* **Board:** K♠ J♠ 5♦ 8♥ 5♣
* **Hero (BB):** A♣ T♣ → **Ace-high**, no pair
* **Pot:** 38 chips
* **Stacks:** Deep (started 100)
* **River action:** SB checks after check/raise on turn
* **Positions:** SB (raiser preflop) vs BB (hero)

---

## 3. Opponent range estimate

### **Preflop – SB raises, BB calls**

SB raising range (3x) includes:

* All broadways (A/K/Q/J combos)
* Strong Ax, some suited A2–A5
* Pairs 22–QQ/KK/AA
* Suited connectors down to 65s
* Some suited gappers

### **Flop (K J 5), SB checks, we check**

SB checking removes most strong top-pair+ hands from his range or indicates pot-control.
SB still has:

* **Medium strength:** Jx, 5x, Kx (often checks some Kx)
* **Draws:** spades, QT, T9
* **Air:** A4/A3/A2, Qx, Tx, small suited connectors

### **Turn (8♥) – SB checks, we bet 5, SB raises to 16, we call**

This is the KEY node.

SB’s check-raise is usually:

* **Strong value:** KJ, K8s, J8s, 55, maybe slow-played sets, maybe 88
* **Draws:**

  * All spade draws
  * QT (open-ender)
  * T9 (open-ender)
  * Q9 sometimes
* **Bluffs:** Some hands like Q4s/Q3s, A2–A4 with a spade blocker

So after the turn check-raise, SB is heavily weighted toward:

* Value > draws >> air

### **River (5♣) – SB checks**

This is important.

Hands that BET river for value:

* KJ, K8, J8, 88, and **all full houses**
  So these are mostly removed.

Hands that CHECK river:

* **Missed draws:**

  * Spades bricked
  * QT (gutshot) missed
  * T9 missed
* **Marginal value / showdown:**

  * Jx
  * Kx without improvement (pot-control)
* **Weak value** that fears raise
* **Air**

SB’s check after a turn check-raise very often means:
**“I have a draw that missed OR a medium strength hand I don’t want to bet.”**

---

## 4. Pot odds and equity estimate

Hero currently has **A-high**, which almost never wins at showdown versus:

* Any pair
* Any Jx
* Any Kx
* Any 8x
* Any 5x
  So our **raw equity if we check is near zero**.

If hero bets, we target:

* Missed **spades**
* Missed **QT**
* Missed **T9**
* Random air floats
  These make up a large part of SB’s river check range after turn check-raise.

We need our bluff to work > **~33%** if we bet around half pot.

---

## 5. Action reasoning

### **Why checking is bad**

* We virtually never win at showdown.
* SB has tons of Jx/Kx that beat Ace-high.
* Equity realization is near zero.

### **Why bluffing is good**

SB’s line = **check flop → check turn → check-raise → check river**
This is often a missed draw line:

**Hands that fold to a bet:**

* QTs (most combos)
* T9s
* Missed spades that didn’t improve
* Low pairs (22–44)
* Ace-high hands
* Some Jx may fold to a large bet because the river paired the board and we represent 5x/sets/boats.

### **Sizing**

To maximize fold equity versus Jx and draws, we need something like:

* **BET 20–28 chips** (55–75% pot)

A small block bet isn’t credible and gets looked up too often by Jx, Kx.

A large bet polarizes us into:

* **Trips / boats** (5x, 55, 88, K5s rarely, A5s)
* **Strong value like KJ**

SB’s check indicates they are not comfortable with the nut range.
Thus, a polarizing bet folds out nearly all of SB’s middling hands.

### **Optimal GTO/Exploit mix:**

This is a **good bluff candidate**:

* We hold **A♣**, blocking some Ace-high call-downs
* We hold **T♣**, blocking T9/T8 that might call small bets
* Our hand has **zero showdown value**

We prefer to bluff this combo over something like Q4s because A♣T♣ blocks call-down ranges better.

---

## 6. Final decision: **BET 24**

A 24-chip bet (≈ 65% pot) maximizes fold equity, gets folds from all draws and many marginal Jx/Kx hands, and is the optimal bluff frequency from this node.

**Final: BET 24**
